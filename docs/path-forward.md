# Path Forward

This document describes how the concept may move from a raw draft toward community adoption. It complements the [main proposal](../README.md).

---

## Collaborative Drafting

The main goal of this project is **collaborative drafting** — not publishing a finished constitution.

The current README is intentionally **raw but concrete**: detailed enough for experienced DReps to assess whether a more fundamental rethink of treasury governance is worth pursuing, yet open enough for direct involvement in shaping the next version.

If the direction finds support, the next stage should bring more DReps into workshops, structured review, and iterative refinement — similar in spirit to a governance conference process where competing ideas are presented, discussed, and merged into a shared draft.

---

## Adoption Options

Two broad paths are possible. They are not mutually exclusive in design terms, but they differ in timing and political risk.

### Option A — Proceed toward full framework adoption

Introduce the annual budget governance model as the primary treasury allocation path when the ecosystem is ready. Requires broad DRep agreement and sufficient tooling preparation.

### Option B — Limited pilot first

Run a **controlled pilot** within a capped budget: annual framework selection, structured categories, preference voting, challenge periods, and structured data — evaluated after one full budget cycle.

A pilot can demonstrate feasibility without committing the entire treasury to a new architecture on day one.

#### Pilot risk: parallel tracks

If the pilot is treated as an optional side channel while the main treasury path continues unchanged, participation may skew toward smaller or experimental actors. Established participants may remain on the existing path by default. The pilot would then fail to test the model under representative conditions, and its results would carry limited weight.

#### Pilot risk: preparation cost and timeline

A credible pilot still requires substantial preparation — templates, tooling, disclosure rules, administrator and auditor roles, and DRep education — comparable in effort to launching a slimmed-down full reform. Because assessment meaningfully happens only after a **full budget year**, a pilot likely adds roughly **two years** before a broader reform decision: one year to run, one to review and decide next steps. During that interval, the current NCL-based process may further normalize as the default, making later change harder rather than easier.

These risks do not rule out a pilot. They mean a pilot should be designed as a **serious, representative test** with explicit success criteria and a committed review milestone — not as an indefinite optional lane.

## Staged Evolution

Not every component needs to ship in year one.

| Component | Suggested phase |
| --------- | ---------------- |
| Annual framework and categories | Pilot or year 1 |
| Structured proposals and Option 0 | Pilot or year 1 |
| Preference voting (ranked choice) | Pilot or year 1 |
| Challenge and refinement process | Pilot or year 1 |
| Vendor reputation and institutional memory | **Year 2+** — design separately once core procurement flow is stable |
| On-chain reputation records | Year 2+ |
| Advanced voting extensions | Only after core methods prove stable (see [Preference Voting](preference-voting.md)) |

Reputation in particular attracts scrutiny and design debate. Treating it as a **staged module** keeps the first adoption question focused on structure and procurement fairness rather than on scoring mechanics.

## Legal and Compliance Review

Several DReps raised questions about **legal exposure** — for example, how constitutional approval of an administrator role relates to DRep-only trust allocation, or how performance records affect accountability if mismanagement is alleged.

This concept does not resolve those questions. They depend on jurisdiction, constitutional text, and Cardano Foundation or community legal interpretation.

**Recommendation:** once the ecosystem signals interest in proceeding, engage **legal SME reviewers** to track the evolving design and flag where proposed roles, votes, or records may align or conflict with liability, treasury, or governance law. That review belongs **after** the basic architecture earns support — not as a blocker to discussing the idea, but as a required step before constitutional or operational hardening.

## Deliberately Deferred Detail

The following items are valuable but belong in a **more evolved stage** if the project gains traction — not in the raw concept draft:

* International calendar anchoring for fixed annual dates
* Anti-spam rules for framework submission floods
* Emergency or security contingency pots within the annual budget
* Mid-year administrator handover edge cases
